# Sci-fi & Modern Kit

A snap-together kit for space stations, labs, bunkers and modern interiors on the **1 m grid** —
the second world style next to the medieval **Modular Architecture Kit**, on the **same grid and
the same pivots**, so the two kits (and props-kit) mix in one scene. Clean off-white painted
panels, warm gunmetal, and **teal** as the one accent (the same teal as props-kit's sci-fi crate
and console); the light strips, screens and pads really **emit**. Every piece is measured, not
eyeballed: its size below is exact to the millimetre, and pieces placed on the grid meet with
**no gaps** — the pack's e2e builds a two-room station and counts background pixels through the
joints (zero).

## The grid in one paragraph

**Walls stand ON grid lines, centred on them.** A wall is 2 m long, 3 m tall (one storey) and
0.25 m thick, and its pivot is its **bottom centre**. So a wall placed at `x = 1` runs from
`x = 0` to `x = 2`, half its thickness on each side of the line. Turn it 90° and place it at
`z = 1` and it runs along the other axis. **Floor and ceiling tiles fill the 2 × 2 m cells**
(pivot bottom centre, so a tile at `(1, 0, 1)` covers `0..2 × 0..2`; a ceiling at `(1, 3, 1)`
sits on the wall tops). **Posts and pillars sit on the grid points** where lines cross. With
1 m snapping every pivot lands on whole metres.

```
   z=0  ─────[wall x=1]─────┬─────[wall x=3]─────        walls on the lines,
        │                   │                   │        posts on the points,
   [wall z=1, 90°]   tile (1,0,1)   tile (3,0,1)         tiles in the cells
        │                   │                   │
   z=2  ┼───────────────────┼───────────────────┼
```

**Wall-mounted pieces share the wall's pivot.** The wall screen, the vent, the sliding door and
the window glass are modelled in the wall's own frame: give them the **same position and
rotation as the wall** and they sit on its **+Z face** (turn them 180° for the other face).

## Snapping in the app

1. Open the Explorer → **Packs** → **Sci-fi & Modern Kit**; double-click a piece (it appears at
   the origin) or drag it into the viewport.
2. Right-click the viewport → **Snapping** → **Enable snapping**, then **Position 1** and
   **Rotation 90°**.
3. Move pieces with the gizmo: they jump from grid point to grid point. Rotate walls by 90° to
   turn a corner.
4. Storeys are 3 m apart: ceilings at `y = 3`, the next floor's walls at `y = 3` too.

Then furnish it freely: consoles and racks with their backs to a wall, a **Wall screen** or
**Vent** at a wall's own position/rotation (turned to face into the room), a teleport pad in the
middle of a room, pipe runs along the walls.

### Doors and windows

The doorway is exactly **1.2 × 2.4 m** and the window opening **1.4 × 1.0 m with its sill at
1.0 m**, both centred in the wall. **Wall — open doorway** is lined (lit jambs); put the
**Sliding door** at the same position and rotation to close it, or use **Wall + sliding door**
(both in one piece). **Wall + window** has its glass and frame built in.

### Pipes

**Pipe run, straight** is 2 m along X with its pivot in the middle, so, like a wall, it goes on a
grid line at an odd metre; its flanges meet the next pipe's on the whole metres. **Pipe run,
elbow** has its pivot on the **corner grid point**: its legs end 1 m out at −X and +Z, where the
next straight pipes (pivots 2 m further out) begin. The pipe axis is 0.2 m up — it rests on a
floor tile when placed at `y = 0.1`.

### Storeys, stairs and ramps

**Stairs** climb one storey (3 m) over a 4 m run, 2 m wide, rising towards −Z. Put their pivot
at the centre of a 2 × 4 m slot and the top tread meets a floor at `y = 3`; the handrails run
on 0.46 m above that floor (the piece is 3.455 m tall). The **Ramp** rises
1 m over a 2 × 2 cell (low edge at +Z) — the same size as architecture-kit's, so it lands on
that kit's Block. **Railings** go on floor edges like walls.

### Mixing with the Modular Architecture Kit

Both kits use one grid: a sci-fi wall swaps one-for-one with a sandstone wall, the floors are
the same 0.1 m, the storeys the same 3 m — a stone ruin with a sci-fi bunker inside, or a
modern annex on a medieval hall, snaps together without adapters.

### Scene budget

Every placed piece carries its own textures (the app does not share them between copies), and
the app asks "This model is heavy" once a scene's textures pass **512 MB** of GPU memory. So the
pieces that repeat — walls, doorways, windows, corner posts, floors, ceilings, pillars, the door —
use **512² maps (~4 MB each)**; the props use 1024² maps (~16-21 MB each). A furnished two-room
station (37 shell pieces + 20 props) is about 425 MB: build big stations mostly out of
architecture, and dress them with props where the camera goes.

## How the pieces were made

Generated with Meshy.ai (text-to-3D, then textured with PBR maps) and post-processed by
`tools/scifi-kit/` in this repo: every mesh is simplified to its triangle budget, stretched to
its exact size, given its pivot, and its joint faces are made exactly planar; the doorway,
window and corner post are CUT from the one wall so their openings are exact; a solid core seals
each wall and floor; the teal light strips Meshy painted into a texture become the emissive map.
Pieces whose value is an exact shape — grating, ceiling light, ramp, railing, pipes, vent, wall
screen, light column, window glass, doorway lining — are built procedurally (0 credits).
`kit.json` there is the whole recipe (`node tools/scifi-kit/build.mjs` rebuilds the pack).
