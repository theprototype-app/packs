# Modular Architecture Kit

A snap-together kit for houses, halls, towers, dungeons and courtyards on the **1 m grid**.
Three material families that mix: **sandstone** (walls, floors, stairs, tower), **oak** (doors,
beams, fences, railings) and **slate** (roofs), with **oxblood** paint as the accent (doors,
shutters, the gate). Every piece is measured, not eyeballed: its size below is exact to the
millimetre, and pieces placed on the grid meet with **no gaps** — the pack's e2e builds a sealed
room and counts background pixels through the joints (zero).

## The grid in one paragraph

**Walls stand ON grid lines, centred on them.** A wall is 2 m long, 3 m tall (one storey) and
0.25 m thick, and its pivot is its **bottom centre**. So a wall placed at `x = 1` runs from
`x = 0` to `x = 2`, half its thickness on each side of the line. Turn it 90° and place it at
`z = 1` and it runs along the other axis. **Floor tiles fill the 2 × 2 m cells** (pivot bottom
centre, so a tile at `(1, 0, 1)` covers `0..2 × 0..2`). **Posts and pillars sit on the grid
points** where lines cross. With 1 m snapping every pivot lands on whole metres.

```
   z=0  ─────[wall x=1]─────┬─────[wall x=3]─────        walls on the lines,
        │                   │                   │        posts on the points,
   [wall z=1, 90°]   tile (1,0,1)   tile (3,0,1)         tiles in the cells
        │                   │                   │
   z=2  ┼───────────────────┼───────────────────┼
```

## Snapping in the app

1. Open the Explorer → **Packs** → **Modular Architecture Kit**; double-click a piece (it
   appears at the origin) or drag it into the viewport.
2. Right-click the viewport → **Snapping** → **Enable snapping**, then **Position 1** and
   **Rotation 90°**. (Position 0.5 works too — every pivot is on whole or half metres.)
3. Move pieces with the gizmo: they jump from grid point to grid point. Rotate walls by 90°
   to turn a corner.
4. Build storeys 3 m apart: the upper floor's tiles and walls go at `y = 3`, the next at `y = 6`.

### Doors and windows

The doorway is exactly **1.0 × 2.2 m** and the window opening **0.8 × 1.0 m with its sill at
1.1 m**, both centred in the wall. The **Door** and **Window** pieces share the wall's pivot:
give them the wall's position and rotation and they sit in the opening, their frames covering
the cut edges.

### Roofs

A **Roof slope** is 2 m along the ridge, 2 m of run and 1.5 m of rise (37°); its low eave faces
+Z. Two rows back to back (0° and 180°) make a gable roof over a 4 m-deep building; the
**Gable** half-triangles close the ends (one at 90°, its partner at −90°). A longer hall just
repeats slopes and gables every 2 m.

### Storeys and stairs

**Stairs** climb one storey (3 m) over a 4 m run, 2 m wide, rising towards −Z. Put their pivot
at the centre of a 2 × 4 m slot and the top step meets a floor tile at `y = 3`.

## How the pieces were made

Generated with Meshy.ai (text-to-3D, then textured with PBR maps) and post-processed by
`tools/architecture-kit/` in this repo: every mesh is simplified to 1-6k triangles, stretched to its
exact size, given its pivot, and its joint faces are made exactly planar; the doorway, window,
half-wall, gable and corner-post pieces are CUT from the one wall so their openings are exact;
the brick walls get a solid mortar core so no light leaks through their joints. `kit.json`
there is the whole recipe (`node tools/architecture-kit/build.mjs` rebuilds the pack).
